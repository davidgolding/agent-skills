# AP Stylebook

Condensed guidance for mechanical/copy/reference passes; the manual governs. AP is a news-writing style--no formal footnote/bibliography architecture; sourcing is handled in-text by attribution, not citation apparatus.

- **Serial comma:** no, omitted before the final "and"/"or" in a simple series unless needed for clarity.
- **Quotation marks:** double for first-level, single for nested; standard American placement (commas/periods inside).
- **Dashes:** em dash set with spaces on both sides (AP convention differs from Chicago/APA here); used for abrupt change or emphasis.
- **Numbers:** spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above; always use numerals for ages, dimensions, percentages, and addresses.
- **Dates/time:** month abbreviated with a specific day ("Jan. 5"), not abbreviated when standing alone or with just a year ("January 2026"). Time as "10 a.m.," no colons for on-the-hour.
- **Capitalization:** sentence-case for most headlines (varies by house); formal titles capitalized only immediately before a name.
- **Abbreviations:** AP has an extensive, specific abbreviation list (states, months, titles)--treat any abbreviation call not obviously covered here as needing a style-sheet ruling.
- **Italics:** AP style traditionally avoids italics (wire-service legacy); quotation marks used for titles of works instead. Confirm with the user whether the house has departed from this default.
- **Sourcing:** in-text attribution ("according to," "X said") rather than a citation apparatus; no locator format applies. If reference-list-style citation is actually wanted, confirm the user doesn't mean Chicago or APA instead.
