# Rhetoric Patterns & Anti-Patterns

This document defines the analysis patterns and anti-patterns used by rhetorician. The figure catalog itself lives in `references/figures.md`.

## Patterns

- **Name**: Catalog-Grounded Identification
- **When**: While naming any figure in a passage — attach the identification to the catalog entry that defines it rather than to general impression.
- **Example**: ```
  Look up the candidate term in references/figures.md. Name the figure using
  the term the catalog uses as its **Name**, and let the catalog's
  **Definition** be the test the passage segment has to satisfy. Where the
  segment satisfies no entry's definition, describe the device and say the
  catalog does not name it.
  ```

---

- **Name**: Structured Commentary Entry
- **When**: While writing each finding in the analysis output.
- **Example**: ```
  **"I came, I saw, I conquered"**: The three clauses arrive without
  connectives, so each lands as a separate completed act and the sequence
  gathers speed toward the last verb ([[#asyndeton]]).

  Shape: bold quoted segment, colon, commentary on the effect in this
  passage, inline citation of the catalog entry in parentheses.
  ```

---

- **Name**: Segment-First Scanning
- **When**: At the start of every analysis, before naming any figure.
- **Example**: ```
  Read the passage once for sense. Then mark the segments that depart from
  plain statement — repetitions, inversions, substitutions, omissions,
  addresses, questions, and constructions that strain ordinary syntax.
  Identify figures for the marked segments. Segments that read as plain
  statement carry no figure and need no entry.
  ```

---

- **Name**: Nested Figure Reporting
- **When**: Whenever one marked segment satisfies more than one catalog entry.
- **Example**: ```
  "Nihil ne te nocturnum praesidium palatii, nihil urbis vigilae, nihil
  timor populi..." satisfies anaphora (repeated "nihil"), asyndeton (no
  connectives), and a periodic construction at once.

  Report these as one finding naming the related figures and how they
  combine, rather than as three findings on the same words.
  ```

---

- **Name**: Genus-Species Precision
- **When**: While choosing between a general term and a more specific one that both fit the segment.
- **Example**: ```
  A verb governing several clauses from the middle position satisfies
  [[#zeugma]] (the genus) and [[#mesozeugma]] (the species). Name the
  species, and name the genus alongside it when the reader needs the
  broader category to follow the point.
  ```

---

- **Name**: Vice Identification Without Correction
- **When**: While identifying an entry drawn from the Vices section of the catalog.
- **Example**: ```
  Name the vice, quote the segment, and explain the fault — a solecism is
  reported as a solecism. Supply a corrected rewrite only where the user
  asked for one; an analysis request is answered with an identification,
  not with an edit to the passage.
  ```

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **Name**: Over-Identification
- **Why**: Naming a figure for any incidental repetition or ordinary word order turns the analysis into a list of accidents of plain prose, burying the segments where a figure is genuinely at work and inflating the reader's sense of how figured the passage is.
- **Instead**: Segment-First Scanning — identify figures only for segments that depart from plain statement.

---

- **Name**: Nearest-Label Substitution
- **Why**: Reaching for the closest catalog term when no entry actually fits the segment produces a confident citation for a figure that is not present, which is harder for a reader to catch than an admission that the catalog has no term for the device.
- **Instead**: Catalog-Grounded Identification — describe the device and state that the catalog does not name it.

---

- **Name**: Uncited Assertion
- **Why**: A named figure with no catalog citation and no quoted segment leaves the reader unable to check the identification against either the passage or the authority, which is the whole content of a rhetorical analysis.
- **Instead**: Structured Commentary Entry.

---

- **Name**: Analysis as Editing
- **Why**: Rewriting, paraphrasing, or correcting the passage answers a question the user did not ask and destroys the object under analysis; a figure can only be pointed at in the words that carry it.
- **Instead**: Vice Identification Without Correction — quote and name, and leave the passage's wording intact.

---

- **Name**: Fragmented Overlap
- **Why**: Reporting each figure in a densely figured segment as a separate finding implies the devices are unrelated and makes the passage read as a pile of ornaments rather than as one construction doing several things at once.
- **Instead**: Nested Figure Reporting.

---

- **Name**: Vice-as-Absence
- **Why**: Treating a figure's mere presence as a fault — reporting alliteration or synecdoche as something to be removed — misreads ornament as error. The catalog separates the two: a figure becomes a vice through misuse, not through appearing.
- **Instead**: Identify from the Figures section by default, and draw on the Vices section when the segment shows the specific misuse that entry's definition describes.
