# Evidence Base: HIIT & Interval Training

Curated 2026-09-07. Load only when the request concerns high-intensity intervals, conditioning circuits, or sprint work. If a request cites a specific recent study, a named novel protocol not covered here, or a claim that seems to contradict this file, perform a targeted lookup and flag any contradiction found.

## What qualifies as HIIT

- Repeated efforts at an intensity that is hard-to-maximal (well above the first ventilatory/lactate threshold), separated by incomplete or complete recovery — distinct from steady-state cardio at any intensity, and distinct from moderate-intensity continuous work that is neither easy nor truly hard.
- Protocol structure (work:rest ratio, interval duration, total intervals) is highly variable across supported approaches; shorter, more intense intervals (e.g., 20-40 second efforts) and longer, slightly less intense intervals (e.g., 2-4 minute efforts) both produce meaningful cardiorespiratory adaptation — protocol choice should follow the user's preference, joint tolerance, and equipment more than a claim that one specific protocol is uniquely superior.

## Adaptations and dose

- HIIT produces cardiorespiratory and metabolic adaptations (VO2max, mitochondrial density, insulin sensitivity) efficiently relative to time invested, which is its main practical advantage over equivalent-adaptation steady-state volume.
- The dose-response relationship plateaus — beyond roughly 2-3 quality HIIT sessions per week, additional sessions produce diminishing returns and rising injury/overtraining risk, particularly when layered on top of concurrent strength or endurance volume.
- Completion quality (holding the prescribed intensity across all intervals) is a better indicator of session value than raw interval count; degrading intervals late in a session signal the prescription outran current recovery, not that more intervals should be added (see `references/progression.md`).

## Interference with strength training

- HIIT and heavy strength work compete for the same recovery resources; frequent HIIT sessions in close proximity to heavy lower-body strength days measurably blunt strength adaptation and raise perceived fatigue.
- Spacing HIIT at least a day away from the heaviest strength session in a training week, and capping weekly HIIT frequency against the week's total strength volume, meaningfully reduces this interference — this is the basis for the frequency-ceiling rule in `references/progression.md` and the reason HIIT placement is checked against `program.toon` rather than scheduled independently.

## Populations and caution

- HIIT's high cardiovascular and orthopedic demand makes the medical screen and contraindication check (see `references/safety.md`) especially load-bearing before prescribing it — cardiac flags in particular warrant excluding or delaying HIIT until cleared.
- Deconditioned or return-from-injury trainees benefit from building an aerobic base and completing several weeks of lower-intensity work before introducing HIIT, rather than starting there.
