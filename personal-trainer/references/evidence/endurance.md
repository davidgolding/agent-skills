# Evidence Base: Endurance & Zone 2 Cardio

Curated 2026-09-07. Load only when the request concerns steady-state cardio, aerobic base work, or endurance events. If a request cites a specific recent study, a named novel protocol not covered here, or a claim that seems to contradict this file, perform a targeted lookup and flag any contradiction found.

## Intensity distribution

- Elite endurance athletes across many sports converge on a "polarized" distribution — roughly 75-80% of training volume at low intensity (easy, conversational effort) and most of the remainder at high intensity, with comparatively little time in the moderate "gray zone" (the pattern most associated with Stephen Seiler's research on training intensity distribution).
- For recreational trainees the exact ratio matters less than the principle: most weekly aerobic volume should be easy enough to sustain frequently without accumulating excessive fatigue, with a smaller, deliberate dose of harder work layered on top (often via HIIT — see that file).

## Zone 2 / aerobic base

- "Zone 2" generally refers to an intensity sustainable while breathing comfortably through the nose or holding a conversation — physiologically, an intensity below the first ventilatory/lactate threshold where fat oxidation and mitochondrial adaptation are favored without accumulating significant fatigue.
- Aerobic base work at this intensity is well supported for improving submaximal economy, fat utilization, and recovery capacity, and is the appropriate default for most easy-day cardio regardless of the athlete's primary goal (including as a recovery-compatible addition to a strength-focused program).
- Duration progresses before intensity for this kind of work — extending time at the same easy effort is the primary lever, not speeding up (see `references/progression.md`).

## Progression and monitoring

- Heart-rate drift at a constant pace within a session (heart rate climbing while pace holds steady) is a practical, low-cost signal that a session's target outran current recovery or heat/hydration status; it is a reasonable autoregulation trigger even without lab-derived thresholds.
- Total weekly duration/volume is generally a more load-bearing variable than any single session's intensity for aerobic adaptation — consistency across weeks matters more than any one hard effort.

## Interference with strength training

- Endurance volume can blunt concurrent strength and hypertrophy adaptation, with the effect scaling with the volume and frequency of endurance work relative to strength work; moderate endurance volumes (a few sessions per week at low-to-moderate intensity) generally coexist reasonably well with a strength-focused block.
- Same-day sequencing (strength first, endurance after, or well-separated by time of day) reduces acute interference relative to endurance immediately preceding strength work.
