# Evidence Base: Mobility & Yoga

Curated 2026-09-07. Load only when the request concerns flexibility, mobility work, or yoga. If a request cites a specific recent study, a named novel method not covered here, or a claim that seems to contradict this file, perform a targeted lookup and flag any contradiction found.

## Mobility vs flexibility

- Flexibility (passive range of motion) and mobility (active, controlled range of motion under load or in a movement pattern) are related but distinct; training aimed at improving performance or reducing injury risk should generally prioritize mobility (control through range) over passive flexibility alone.
- Static stretching held for meaningful duration (commonly cited in the range of 30+ seconds per position, accumulated over multiple sets) reliably improves passive range of motion over weeks of consistent practice.

## Timing relative to training

- Static stretching immediately before strength or power work can produce a small, transient reduction in force and power output; dynamic mobility work (active range-of-motion drills, movement-specific warm-ups) is the better default immediately pre-session, with static stretching more appropriately placed post-session or on separate recovery days.
- Yoga and longer mobility flows fit well as their own session or as active recovery on non-lifting days, rather than as a substitute warm-up immediately before heavy strength work.

## Progression

- Progression in this domain is range of motion, hold duration, and movement complexity — not external load (see `references/progression.md`). A useful marker of readiness to progress is achieving the current target without compensation (e.g., substituting lumbar flexion for hip mobility in a hinge stretch).
- Discomfort consistent with a normal stretch sensation is expected and appropriate; sharp, pinching, or radiating sensations are not, and should regress the position immediately rather than being pushed through — this maps to the general regression rule and to the sharp-pain red flag in `references/safety.md`.

## Interaction with other training

- Mobility work is generally recovery-compatible and can be placed flexibly within a week without materially competing with strength or cardio adaptation, unlike HIIT or additional endurance volume.
- Targeted mobility work addressing a specific limitation (e.g., ankle dorsiflexion for squat depth, thoracic extension for overhead position) has more carryover to lifting performance than generic full-body flexibility routines, and is worth prioritizing when a specific movement limitation has been identified.
